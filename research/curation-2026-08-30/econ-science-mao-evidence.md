## New science source evidence and reading-unit proposals

### `science-lavoisier-conservation-balance`

- Primary source: [Project Gutenberg, Robert Kerr's 1790 translation of *Elements of Chemistry*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30775/30775-h/30775-h.htm).
- Original: “We may lay it down as an incontestible axiom, that, in all the operations of art and nature, nothing is created; an equal quantity of matter exists both before and after the experiment; the quality and quantity of the elements remain precisely the same; and nothing takes place beyond changes and modifications in the combination of these elements.”
- Exact locator: Part I, Chapter XIII, print pp. 130–131, complete continuous sentence.
- Passage context: follows the question of how a sugary vegetable oxide yields carbonic acid and alcohol; the following pages reconcile weighed inputs and products through five tables.
- Natural chapter boundary: Chapter XIII, “Of the Decomposition of Vegetable Oxyds by the Vinous Fermentation,” pp. 129–140.
- Reading unit: embed the complete chapter; it is compact, public domain, and the tables are necessary to see conservation become procedure rather than slogan.
- Illustration prompt: AGORA editorial illustration, a sealed eighteenth-century glass fermentation vessel on a balance; grapes enter at left while gas, clear liquid, and residue exit at right, every material stream connected and the balance level, parchment, amber, charcoal and pale blue, no portrait, no alchemical glyphs, no text, 4:5.
- Batch anchor: `research/illustration-generation/curation-2026-08-30/anchors/science-lavoisier-anchor.png`.

### `science-lavoisier-nomenclature-facts-ideas-words`

- Primary source: same [1790 Kerr edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30775/30775-h/30775-h.htm).
- Original: “The impossibility of separating the nomenclature of a science from the science itself, is owing to this, that every branch of physical science must consist of three things; the series of facts which are the objects of the science, the ideas which represent these facts, and the words by which these ideas are expressed.”
- Exact locator: “Preface of the Author,” print pp. xiv–xv, complete continuous sentence.
- Passage context: follows Condillac's maxims on language and analysis; followed by the three-impressions seal metaphor and Lavoisier's experimental ordering from known to unknown.
- Natural chapter boundary / reading unit: embed the complete author's preface, pp. xiii–xxii.
- Illustration prompt: AGORA editorial illustration, three aligned impressions from one scientific seal—an observed glass vessel, a clean abstract diagram, and a set of blank typographic blocks—each correcting the other, eighteenth-century paper and restrained brass, no readable letters, no portrait, no text, 4:5.
- Batch anchor: `research/illustration-generation/curation-2026-08-30/anchors/science-lavoisier-anchor.png`.
